Last week, the 0.5 mm aluminium sheet dropped because a 1 bar vacuum couldn't flatten the badly formed sheet, and gluing aluminium to wood over 390 mm failed due to thermal expansion differences. This time, we're curing in an autoclave at 3 to 7 bar with a silicone plug pressing every face, which is 5 to 10 times the pressure needed to flatten the sheet. The sheet isn't glued; it’s held by one screw in the middle and two in 3 mm slots with release agent underneath, allowing it to slide every cure and stay elastic. We’re using H14 half-hard temper, as soft O temper would yield and wrinkle. The Caltech group we're copying uses aluminium tools, so an aluminium face keeps us closer to their process. It also seals the machined plywood end grain, ensuring the laminate sits on a smooth, non-porous face and preventing wood moisture from reaching it. The wood is cut 0.5 mm smaller for the arc radius.
