### Findings

1. **BLOCKER: Tool Face and Wood Arc Alignment**
   - **Evidence**: The tool face is defined at `R18.0, web land x = -0.20, tip land y = 18.0`, while the wood arc is defined at `R17.5` with the same center `(-18.20, 0)`. The tool face is not perfectly aligned with the wood arc, which may cause misalignment issues during the forming process.
   - **Fix**: Ensure that the tool face and wood arc are perfectly aligned by setting the tool face radius to `R17.5` and adjusting the web land and tip land accordingly.

2. **HIGH: Pressure Margin Analysis**
   - **Evidence**: The pressure margin analysis is overly conservative, with the pressure on the bag being `1.3-10.7` times higher than necessary. This could indicate a design margin that is not necessary, potentially leading to an unnecessarily tight fit.
   - **Fix**: Re-evaluate the pressure requirements and adjust the design to ensure a more conservative but practical margin. For instance, a pressure range of `1.0-2.0` times the necessary pressure could be more appropriate.

3. **HIGH: Thermal Stress and Friction Analysis**
   - **Evidence**: The thermal stress analysis shows that the friction force the wood can put into the liner is significantly higher than the yield strength of the 1050A-O and 1050A-H14 materials. This could lead to unwanted deformation or even failure of the liner.
   - **Fix**: Use a lower friction coefficient (`μ = 0.1` instead of `μ = 0.3`) or apply additional measures to reduce friction, such as using a lubricant on the liner or modifying the contact surface.

4. **MEDIUM: Web Land Edge Clearance**
   - **Evidence**: The web land edge is left free to stop `0.5 mm` above the bleed gallery, which may not provide enough clearance to ensure a smooth forming process.
   - **Fix**: Increase the clearance above the bleed gallery to at least `1.0 mm` to prevent potential blockages and ensure a smoother forming process.

### Summary

- **BLOCKER**: Align the tool face with the wood arc.
- **HIGH**: Re-evaluate and adjust the pressure margin.
- **HIGH**: Reduce friction to avoid thermal stress issues.
