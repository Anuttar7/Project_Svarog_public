Last week we dropped the 0.5 mm aluminium sheet for two reasons. A vacuum bag at 1 bar could not press a badly formed sheet flat, and gluing aluminium to wood over 390 mm fails because aluminium grows about 0.75 mm more than plywood over the cure. Now the part cures in an autoclave at 3 to 7 bar with a silicone plug pressing every face, which is 5 to 10 times the pressure needed to flatten the springback onto the wood. So the sheet goes back, unglued: one screw in the middle, two in 3 mm slots, release agent underneath, H14 half-hard temper so it stays elastic. The Caltech group we copy cured on aluminium tools, and the sheet seals the plywood end grain. The cost is cutting the wood 0.5 mm smaller, arc radius 17.5 for an 18.0 face.
