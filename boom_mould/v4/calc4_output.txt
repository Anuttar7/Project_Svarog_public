## 1. Spring-in: calc3 had the sign backwards
Leclerc et al. 2017 (mould radius) and 2018 (cured shape) [src]: tool R12.7 at 90 deg cured to R10.6 at 105 deg
  cured/tool radius = 0.8346  (repo Sizing Script.py carries 0.835: the same number) [der]
  cured/tool angle  = 1.1667: the flange CLOSES, the radius SHRINKS [der]
  arc length tool 19.95 -> cured 19.43 mm (-2.6%): fibres hold it [der]
calc3 said spring-in makes the cured radius LARGER and cut R14.83. Measured on this exact
process it goes the other way. A tool at R14.83 would cure to about R12.4: 17% undersize.
tool face if we spring in like Leclerc (ultra-thin UD, 0.05-0.07 mm): R17.97, part trimmed at 77.1 deg of arc [der]
tool face if we spring in like Radford predicts for a thicker laminate: R15.17 [der]
Our 0.2 mm woven flange sits between the two. Nobody has measured it.
CUT ORDER: a convex corner of larger radius lies wholly outside a smaller one with the same
tangent point (checked on 400k points, review v4). But if the curvature CHANGE is constant the tool
needs R19.6, above R18. So tomorrow: cure coupon pairs at (15.2, 16.5, 18.0, 19.6) and only ROUGH the long blocks;
finish their arc after the coupons are read, after the bake (rough, bake to constant mass, finish, seal).

## 3. Where the silicone plug reaches, and the merge point
   1.00 mm above the web root: gap between the two laminates 0.06 mm
   2.00 mm above the web root: gap between the two laminates 0.22 mm

  10.00 mm above the web root: gap between the two laminates 6.07 mm
  silicone fills down to ~3.0 mm above the root (gap >= 0.5 mm) [der]
  Below that, down to the root, neither tool fully reaches: the flanges only touch at y = 0. Cast
  the plug over a measured 0.22-0.25 mm spacer so, pressurised, it extrudes into this zone; cut and
  section the first coupon there before trusting it (review v4 blocker 2). A rigid wood top piece
  with a 3 mm tongue tip would stop at 7.2 mm: worse, and it bottoms out. [der]

## 4. Blanks: no glueline may cross a forming face
  above y = 0 the block is R = 18.0 tall, so a horizontal glueline anywhere in 0..18.0 cuts the arc.
  Best: ONE strip of 35 mm phenolic birch per block, sheet parallel to the web land, ripped
  60 tall: no glueline at all, machined to 30.0 wide. [assume, review v4 HIGH]
  Fallback 2 x 18 mm the same way: one glueline at x = -18.20, beyond the part's trim
  line (arc ends in the part at x = -14.2); glue with an epoxy whose post-cured Tg is above
  the cure temperature. A PVA or PU wood glue creeps at 120 C and the joint will move. [der]
  Keep one phenolic film face as the web land.

## 5. Pressure in the autoclave
  3 bar gauge + vacuum = 0.40 MPa on the bag:
    plug: ~0.40 MPa normal to every laminate face it touches, root walls included [der]
    blocks' outer faces push them together with 5.6 kN, i.e. 1.4 MPa on the web
    if the shims are not bearing (net area ratio 3.6: outer face 54 less the plug push 18; base friction lowers it further) [der]
  7 bar gauge + vacuum = 0.80 MPa on the bag:
    plug: ~0.80 MPa normal to every laminate face it touches, root walls included [der]
    blocks' outer faces push them together with 11.2 kN, i.e. 2.9 MPa on the web
    if the shims are not bearing (net area ratio 3.6: outer face 54 less the plug push 18; base friction lowers it further) [der]
  So, as Caltech does, the bolts CLAMP: 5 x M6 at 2 N m (~1.5 kN each, ~2 MPa on the web),
  set from the coupons. The butt faces are relieved so the web lands are the only contact,
  and the 0.36 mm stops sit at the trim ends, in line with the web, so they cannot tip the blocks.
  The autoclave adds the load above on top of the bolts, which also covers bolt relaxation in hot wood.

## 6. Silicone plug growth at cure (free surface under the bag)
  120 C: unrestrained plug grows 11.7 mm over 390; an Al spine grows 0.9 [der]
  150 C: unrestrained plug grows 15.2 mm over 390; an Al spine grows 1.2 [der]
  180 C: unrestrained plug grows 18.7 mm over 390; an Al spine grows 1.4 [der]
  An unrestrained plug sliding 7-20 mm along a wet layup can drag the plies. Cast an aluminium
  flat bar into the plug's top as a spine, drilled so the silicone locks through it (tin-cure
  silicone does not bond to bare Al). NO FEP sheet across the V: it bridges the cusp like the bag did;
  silicone releases from epoxy on its own. Use FEP only on the tip lands.
