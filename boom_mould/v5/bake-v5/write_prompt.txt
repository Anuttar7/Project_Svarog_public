It explains why the 0.5 mm aluminium sheet goes back on the wooden mould blocks after last week's version dropped it. Facts (use only these):
- Last version dropped the sheet for two reasons: a vacuum bag at 1 bar could not press a badly formed sheet flat, and gluing aluminium to wood over 390 mm fails because aluminium grows about 0.75 mm more than plywood over the cure.
- Now the part cures in an autoclave at 3 to 7 bar with a silicone plug pressing every face. That is 5 to 10 times the pressure needed to flatten the sheet's springback onto the wood.
- The sheet is not glued. It is held by one screw in the middle and two screws in 3 mm slots, with release agent under it, so it slides every cure and stays elastic. Use H14 half-hard temper; soft O temper would yield and wrinkle.
- The Caltech group whose spring-in measurement (cured radius = 0.835 of tool radius) we copy cured on aluminium tools, so an aluminium face keeps us closer to their process.
- It also seals the machined plywood end grain, so the laminate sits on a smooth non-porous face and wood moisture cannot reach it.
- Cost: the wood is cut 0.5 mm smaller (arc radius 17.5 for an 18.0 tool face).
